Bike-sharing operators trigger rebalancing from live data feeds, and those
feeds degrade: stations drop out, counters freeze, values jump. This thesis
traces how such degradation in Milan's BikeMi feed propagates from raw data to
a two-hour forecast, a dispatch decision, and its operational cost, and asks
how much of the harm a rule-based safeguard recovers without retraining the
model. Six anomaly classes were injected at four intensities and five seeds
into the test periods of a certified 62-period substrate and passed through a
frozen logistic model and a frozen dispatch threshold. Implausible jumps raised
cost by €1,092 to €6,033 over the 48-hour test window by triggering false-alarm
dispatches, and the safeguard recovered 90–97% of that loss. Silent
misreporting cost less (€4–42) and can be neither detected nor recovered.
Dropouts, frozen counters, stale feeds, and capacity inconsistencies left decisions
essentially unchanged, because they touch little data and the frozen model
responds weakly to changes in station counts. In a pre-registered test, three open-weight
language models warned about corrupted data far more often when given quality
flags, yet mostly still followed the pipeline's recommendation: the assistant
raises the alarm, and the rule repairs the cost.
